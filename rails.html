1984 economics

This page is an experiment. The square is a classroom. The payment rules below stay.

What

1984 is a village square on Testnet 10 where a funded address can spend, mint, name a site, and pass a message. The town on the page is LUMBRIDGE. The books are this square’s own ledger. The coin that moves is tKAS, which is Testnet-10 KAS. A funded test wallet can lock that tKAS in a SquarePeg covenant. POCencept stable and KUSDT stable are the keypad amounts on that lock. They are not dollars. Practice coins and older reserve rows stay on this square’s ledger.

Layer-Kaspa opens a name.kas page from the KNS testnet index. Tor is the picture of an extra layer on the internet: a path beside the ordinary one. Tor hides who is talking to whom. Layer-Kaspa does not. Visitors can open a published page. Only the address that owns the name on that index can change it. The funded test wallet on that page can inscribe more than one name. The first name is the default. domain/address on the balance bar changes it. This square keeps an index of names it inscribed or verified. The KNS testnet index is still the network record. A short private note is ciphertext in a Testnet-10 payload. A longer one stays encrypted on this desk. The page title, welcome, and offers stay readable. No covenant is deployed. A pasted address uses the KNS app.

Kachat on the square is messages only. You paste another kaspatest address, send a handshake, and they accept. Then text moves. Each step is 0.01 POCencept. The chain app at KaChat is a different program: it encrypts on the device and puts the payload in a Kaspa transaction. This desk does not.

Why

A coin you can only hold has done the vault. The use is to pay another person, fast, anywhere. Kaspa is proof of work, so the right to write the next block does not come from already holding the coins. Proof of stake hands that right to capital. That is a different system. Kaspa kept the work and made it fast. The applications on top of that work are still being built. This square is one classroom for that spend, not the finished till.

How

Take a funded address at the gate. Walk to a counter, or open Layer or Kachat on the side rail. A shop buy, a site offer, and a chat step show what they cost before they move a balance. A funded test wallet locks tKAS in a SquarePeg covenant. Redeem, exchange, freeze, and a shop spend of that lock are Layer-1 transactions. A pasted address still sends tKAS to the reserve, because this page cannot sign that covenant, and that tag stays on the ledger. Practice coins stay on the ledger. The miner fee is always KAS. On a tKAS lock that fee is 87 times the standard Testnet 10 rate, and it is extra.

On Layer-Kaspa, open a name you own on the KNS testnet index. The owner can show that name instead of the tKAS address. The owner window sets a title, a tagline, a welcome, a color, one https link, and up to six products or services with a price in cents. A visitor sees that page and can pay an offer. The visitor does not get the owner window. A buyer pays POCencept or KUSDT. The tag moves from their balance to yours. The bank is on that same card, so tKAS can be swapped into a tag first. On Kachat the handshake has to land before a message. The fee is the small POCencept charge. The practice purse in the bank books covers it, and so does a swap.

Goal

The goal is stable money a person can spend anywhere, peer to peer, in Kaspa or in a stable. The bill at the end of that road is a car, an AI service, a game purchase, or a rented service. Wide use is how many tills take the coin. 1984 shows the three rails on one counter so the gap is visible. A promise here does not buy the car, the service, the game, or the rental. The spend gate stays shut.

The stag

The stag is the hunt in Yonatan Sompolinsky’s Project Staghunt. Each hunter can catch a hare alone. The stag is the larger animal, and it lands only if they hold the line together. The essay is the six-pager of 1 April 2026 on hashd.ag. Four ideas sit in it. Coordinated atomicity: the pack pays as one snap, or it does not pay. Accumulation opacity: you do not have to show the whole pile to join. Capital multiplexing: the same stable pile can sit against more than one counterparty until the first snap takes it. Composability: those snaps can be stacked.

The ceiling is a till a stranger can receive on. Kaspa sequences the coin now, because it is proof of work at high frequency. Sequencing the applications on that work is in process. Hunt Hall on the square walks the shape with ledger cents. It does not open that till. The longer note is CEILING.md.

A use for crypto

The best case for crypto is stable money you can spend anywhere. The spend is Kaspa, and Kaspa is volatile, or the spend is a stable. Without that, the coin has no point. Peer to peer is the payment. One person pays another. Five percent of this portfolio is crypto. A profit stays in crypto, in a stable position, to hold or to spend, rather than cashed out to fiat. The use is to spend it, and to use it, fast, anywhere.

Applications and the other utilities matter as much as the coin, and sometimes more. A name, a site, a message, and a till are those utilities in miniature on this square. Kaspa needs both the coin and the applications before it leaves the bubble. Proof of stake offers part of that spend. It does not offer what scalable proof of work offers. That is settled. POCencept stable and KUSDT stable remain tags in this village. They are not that money.

Funded test addresses

One thousand Testnet-10 addresses. Each one was sent 2,000 tKAS. tKAS is Testnet-10 KAS, the coin on this network. With tKAS you can go to the bank. The gate on 1984 takes a funded address. The same browser gets the same funded wallet. Wallet sign-in comes later.

The address and the transaction that funded it are on the funded list. That page stays short: search, or open forty lines at a time. The same file is on GitHub. The balance on each line is read again every 12 hours.

What the square is

1984 is a village square on Testnet 10. The town on the page is LUMBRIDGE. You walk in, you pay at a counter, and the books sit in this square’s own ledger. The spend gate stays shut.

How a payment works

Every payment on the square shows its steps, then the transaction, on the page. The steps are: the page asks, you pay, the receipt is written, and it is done. When it finishes you can open the transaction, or start a new purchase. A buy can be repeated.

A shop, a cinema ticket, a snack, a hop, or a Hunt Hall promise asks on the page, then OK. That buy is this square's ledger. tKAS is Testnet-10 KAS, the coin that moves. With tKAS you can go to the bank and swap it for a ledger tag. That lock is a Testnet 10 transaction. After it is seen, this ledger writes the tag. A funded test address can lock tKAS now. Wallet sign-in comes later.

Three rails, labeled, not welded. KAS is the coin that moves. On this square that label is tKAS. PoC kUSD is POCencept stable, a tag in this village ledger, not a dollar. The Tether-like rail is KUSDT stable, the other tag, and it can be frozen. A practice-coin or older reserve-tag buy has a receipt on this ledger. A SquarePeg shop spend is a Layer-1 covenant transaction, and that share of tKAS goes to the reserve. A tKAS payment that has a txid is the Testnet 10 transaction. Open it on the Testnet 10 explorer. Liquidity records the promise and spends nothing on any rail.

BitCoffee’s KUSD is a different object and is not minted here. The live quote sets a tKAS shop price and the tag written when tKAS is locked. Redeem returns that locked tKAS, in proportion to the locked tag that remains. A later quote does not reprice the lock. PegLab is the classroom where a thin peg breaks. This square is not that peg. It is not a promise of dollars.

The miner fee is always KAS. That is the mining coin. On this square that fee is 87 times the standard Testnet 10 rate, and it is extra. The fee is not POCencept stable and it is not KUSDT stable.

The same three are on every counter, the ticket, the snacks, a hop, and every row in Hunt Hall. tKAS only locks at the bank. POCencept stable redeems and holds the practice purse. KUSDT stable is the only booth with a freeze. A freeze blocks KUSDT spending and swaps. The tKAS already locked on that tag can still be redeemed. The miner fee is extra KAS and is not taken from the lock.

The roadster stays 1.00 on this ledger. If you already have it, another buy of the keys charges again and the car stays yours.

The spending rules

Each address can set a daily cap, a shop list, a rail list, and a confirm line. An empty list means every shop and every rail. The page checks the rule before it moves a balance. Those rules are a stand-in you chose. They live on this page.

A lab beside the square

A lab sits beside this square. It is STP-KAS/kusdt-split. It is early development. It is not a dollar, and it is not this till.

The SilverScript lock has one entry, redeem. The holder signs. The first output pays that same key the full locked amount. The miner fee is another input. The script has no freeze and no blacklist. On 1 Oct 2026 the Kaspa script engine redeemed 1 tKAS to the holder and kept the fee on the other input. A skimmed output failed. A different recipient failed. A different key failed. Nothing was broadcast. There is no Testnet 10 transaction id.

A local program freezes only the KUSDT tag. A POCencept move in that same batch still lands. In the lab that move debits at once. On this square a promise waits for the snap. Using the tag as gas is refused. The program does not hold the coins, and it does not spend the lock. It is not Maxim’s tic-tac-toe guest, and it is not a vProg settlement.

KCC-20 on kccs main 3fbec524 is Last Call, not Final. The header has no Last-Call-Deadline. The state order is amount, owner, owner scheme, borrow scheme, borrow guard, extension commitment. A borrow has to increase the amount. A negative threshold counts as zero. The same extension commitment is one fungible class. The reference that merged with that text is argent-lang/kcc20-reference c8a08711. The master-file pin is 5d7dcc9 on a branch. Main of that file was not moved. The KCC-20 file still writes the name P2PKHHash. KCC-2 defines that value as unkeyed BLAKE3.

On this square a lock checks that increase. POCencept and KUSDT use different extension commitments, so a standard move cannot mix them. A standard move stays within 3 inputs and 3 outputs. Freezing KUSDT changes its commitment, so frozen units are not the liquid units. A shop burn is not a standard transfer: the amount leaves, and the locked share of tKAS is extinguished. That tKAS stays in the reserve. It is not paid out twice. The bank lock mints a POCencept or KUSDT tag only after it locks tKAS. Redeem returns that lock. It is not the reference public mint, which splits a remaining supply with no deposit. KCC-20 does not store a decimal or a ticker. The cent face is this square’s keypad. A funded test wallet locks new tKAS in one SquarePeg covenant. The UTXO value is the locked tKAS. Redeem pays the holder. A shop spend pays the reserve and the tag does not continue. An exchange flips the rail and keeps the sompi. A freeze is KUSDT only and the issuer signs it. A pasted address still uses the reserve, because this page cannot sign that covenant. Practice coins and older reserve rows stay on the ledger. KCC-20 is not Final. This is not a dollar. The spend gate on a stranger's till stays shut.

Mint is the building on the square. What: a named token on this square’s ledger. It is not tKAS, not a dollar, and not a spendable Layer-1 coin. How: open a new name, mint more of an open name, or send some to another kaspatest address. A mint has to increase the holder’s amount. A send keeps the total. The name is hashed with unkeyed BLAKE3, and that hash is the extension. Why: a funded address can try those three options on Testnet 10 while KCC-20 is still Last Call. Type 0 in Cap for no cap. There is no maximum. A whole number above 0 is the most that can be minted. The names POC, POCencept, KUSDT, KAS, and tKAS stay the rails and cannot be opened here. No covenant is deployed.

GitHubs on this matter

**Experimental only. Not a product.**

Do not use wallet integrations on this GitHub. STP remains a clown. This is a delusional desk, not a wallet kit.

None of these GitHub repos are to be taken seriously. Experimental only. Pass logs, classrooms, reviews. Not Kaspa core. Not money. Not an audit. Do not fund, list, or ship them as cash.

  • STP-KAS/kaspa-dappsWhat: the map this argument came from (RAILS.md). Why: one place that tries to stop welding names. Experimental notes, not protocol.
  • STP-KAS/kusdt-splitWhat: a SilverScript lock that pays locked tKAS back to the holder, plus a local program that freezes only the KUSDT tag. Why: the covenant, the tag, and KCC-20 Last Call stay three receipts. Not a dollar. Not BitCoffee. Not a vProg. No Testnet 10 txid.
  • STP-KAS/grok-heavy-showcaseWhat: freeze lab. Native redeem still works after an issuer freeze; guest transfer fails; useAsGas throws NEVER_GAS. Why: it is the executable “don’t make Tether gas” lesson. A test fixture, not a product.
  • STP-KAS/peglab-stpWhat: WILL DEPEG classroom. Tiny pool, admin oracle. Why: so nobody ships a 2 tKAS pool as a dollar. The depeg is the feature.
  • STP-KAS/peglab-pocWhat: Parker-style receipt unit plus PegLab honesty. Why: name ≠ authenticity. Timeout journal still a gap. Not USD.
  • STP-KAS/ishumWhat: self-hosted till. Quote EUR, settle KAS, desk holds 0 keys. kUSD/USDT seats are chairs. Why: the three-rail keypad. A PoC shop, not a payment company.
  • bitcoffee0/kusdWhat: upstream KAS-overcollateral covenant dollar on TN10. Why: the only L1 dollar-named protocol this desk has seen with accepted lifecycle txs. Peg unproven. Unaudited. Not shops.
  • STP-KAS/kusdt-bitcoffeeWhat: independent pass of BitCoffee (txids, not a vibe). Why: so “watch it” has a paper trail. Not an audit. Not an endorsement.
  • NeaBouli/1kUSDWhat: collateralized PSM research, EVM reference. Why: study the design; their own README says not production. Do not race it with a thinner copy.
  • elldeeone/kaspa-x402What: x402 v2 binding for native KAS. Why: the 402 path in the conclusion. Tag is rc, TN10. Not v1. Not mainnet. Bind this envelope or none — still experimental.
  • STP-KAS/sixpack.wtfWhat: this site. Why: it hosts the argument. A personal clown page with a faucet. Not Kaspa core.

Again: experimental only. Do not take any of those repos seriously as money, as Kaspa core, or as something to list.
