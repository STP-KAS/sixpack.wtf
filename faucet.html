Who mines the faucet address

A Testnet-10 miner farm on the Grok Bot Linux sandbox — not Windows, not a phone, not your computer, not this website’s host. You chat with tn10 bot; the node and miners run in that remote Linux box.

How many. Last honest count from the sandbox: alive ≈ 96 miner processes. That is the alive field in /tmp/kaspa-tn10-miners/status.json, not a public API. This page cannot see that file, so the number is the last Grok Bot check — do not treat 96 as live-right-now.

What they mine to. Every process pays the same locked address (never change it): kaspatest:qzffl5xy9np46gkttyuftqnv2w04pr8g3wsp7c3vv8se3txtelx6q7c0v0ldx. Network is Testnet-10 only. Confirmed live on api-tn10 this pass (address exists; tKAS has no value).

Why load is high. The box has 8 CPU cores. The farm is many separate kaspa-miner processes under /tmp/kaspa-tn10-miners/ (miner-001 …), each -t 1, user-agent gbNNN. Load often sits above 100. Extra processes mostly fight for those 8 cores. They do not add real hash 1:1. Toy coins.
